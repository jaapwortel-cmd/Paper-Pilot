Paper Pilot manual
Version 0.0.0.7

Paper Pilot helps you with letters and forms, all by yourself. The program is free and open source, made by Jaap Wortel. It works with NVDA, JAWS and Narrator, with the keyboard and with the mouse.

Read this manual with the arrow keys. Tab moves to the Close button. Escape closes the manual.


1. What Paper Pilot can do

Paper Pilot has two parts.

Read and fill in: for blind and visually impaired people. Scan or open a letter and read it with your screen reader. Fill in forms question by question, then sign and send them.

Check: for sighted people who make a PDF, Word document or website. Paper Pilot tells you in plain language what a screen reader cannot read well, and how to fix it.


2. The main window

At the top is the name Paper Pilot. Below it are two large buttons:
- Read and fill in (shortcut Alt+R)
- Check (shortcut Alt+C)

Use Tab or the arrow keys to move between the buttons. Enter or Space chooses a button. With the mouse, click a button.


3. Reading a document

Choose Read and fill in (Alt+R). Paper Pilot asks: open a file, or scan. If you choose to open a file, the Open document window appears. Choose a PDF, a Word document (.docx or the old .doc), RTF, OpenDocument (.odt, .ods, .odp), Excel (.xlsx), PowerPoint (.pptx), a web page (.html), an e-book (.epub, without copy protection), a text file or a photo or scan (JPG, PNG, TIFF, BMP, GIF, HEIC or WebP) and choose Open.

Paper Pilot turns it into clean text. At the top is the name of the document, and a line with the number of pages, headings and form fields. Read the text with the arrow keys, just like in Notepad. Each page starts with a line such as "Page 1 of 3". Headings are on a line of their own.

Shortcut keys in the reading window:
- Ctrl+O: open another document.
- Ctrl+N: scan.
- Ctrl+I: fill in form.
- Ctrl+P: print.
- Ctrl+F: find. Type or dictate the word and press Enter. F3 finds the next one, Shift+F3 the previous one.
- Ctrl+K: a list of all headings. Choose a heading with the arrow keys and press Enter.
- Ctrl+G: go to a page.
- Ctrl+S: save the text as a text file.
- Ctrl+Shift+S: save a scan or photo (straightened) as a PDF with the text in it, or as a picture.
- Ctrl+H: read handwriting (with a scan or photo).
- Ctrl+D: describe what is on the scan or photo.
- Ctrl+T: What do I need to do? A short summary, and the details for paying.
- Ctrl+Q: QR codes and barcodes that Paper Pilot found.
- Ctrl+R: make a reminder.
- Ctrl+M: email the document as an attachment.
- Escape: close the reading window.
Use Tab to reach the buttons; the same commands are there as well.

If the document is a PDF form with fields, Paper Pilot tells you how many fields it has. Then choose Fill in form (Ctrl+I).

Filling in a form: Paper Pilot asks the questions one by one. First you hear "Question 3 of 12", then the question itself. Type or dictate the answer and press Enter for the next question. With a check box, Space turns it on or off. With a choice, use the arrow keys. Alt+Left arrow goes back to the previous question. Ctrl+L gives an overview of all questions and answers; choose one to change it. Ctrl+S saves the filled-in form as a new PDF. Required questions end with "(required)". For questions such as name, address or BSN, Paper Pilot fills in My details for you; if it is not right, type something else. Escape closes the form window.

Forms without form fields: many forms only have lines and boxes, or are scanned. Choose Fill in form (Ctrl+I) in that case as well. Paper Pilot finds the places to fill in itself: a question with a colon and empty space after it, lines, dots and boxes to check. Filling in then works the same. When saving, Paper Pilot puts your answers in the right place, with a cross in the boxes you checked. This usually works well, but not always: check the filled-in form carefully, or ask someone to check it with you.

Scans and photos: Paper Pilot recognises the printed text, using Windows text recognition and without the internet. If the scan is upside down, on its side or crooked, Paper Pilot tells you and straightens it. With Ctrl+Shift+S you save the straightened scan as a picture (PNG, JPG, TIFF with all pages, BMP or GIF). Handwriting and photos: choose Read handwriting (Ctrl+H) to have handwritten text read, such as a shopping list or postcard. Choose Describe (Ctrl+D) to hear what is on a photo, drawing or scan. This is done by a language model on your own computer, without the internet. The first time, Paper Pilot asks whether you want to download the model (once, about 3.5 GB). The answer appears in a separate window; read it with the arrow keys, Escape closes it. Neat handwriting usually works well, messy handwriting not always. With a graphics card it is fast; with only the processor it can take a few minutes. A word the model cannot read is shown as [unreadable].

What do I need to do? (Ctrl+T): Paper Pilot tells you in plain words who the letter is from, what it is about, what you need to do and by when. The language model on your own computer makes this summary (without the internet). Paper Pilot also finds the amount, the account number (IBAN, checked), the payment reference, deadlines and phone numbers by itself. Then a list appears: choose Read the summary, or copy a detail and paste it with Ctrl+V into your banking app or a website. After copying you return to the list; Escape closes it. Without the language model you only get the details. Please note: Paper Pilot can make mistakes; always check amounts and account numbers against the letter itself.


Reminders (Ctrl+R): Paper Pilot fills in the date and amount if it finds them in the letter. What do I need to do? also has Make a reminder in its list. From three days before the date, Paper Pilot warns you once a day until you choose Done. This also works when Paper Pilot is not open: a scheduled task of Windows checks every day at 10 am. If your computer was off, the reminder comes as soon as it is on again.

Email (Ctrl+M): Paper Pilot creates a new email in your own email program, with the document as an attachment. A scan first becomes a PDF with the text in it. You choose who the email goes to. If your email program does not work together with Paper Pilot (like the new Outlook or Windows Mail), Paper Pilot puts the file on the clipboard and opens a new email: paste the file with Ctrl+V. After saving a filled-in form, Paper Pilot also asks whether you want to email it.

Signature on a form: if you have set up your signature (Settings, My signature), the question for your signature already says [my signature]. When you save, your signature is put there as a picture. Delete the text if you do not want that. Paper Pilot fills in today's date at a question for the date.

QR codes and barcodes: Paper Pilot finds them by itself on scans, photos and PDF pages, and tells you what they contain. A payment QR code (scan to pay) becomes a payment request with name, amount, account number and description. Choose QR codes (Ctrl+Q) to open a link in your browser or copy something. A link never opens by itself: Paper Pilot asks first.

Envelopes: Paper Pilot recognises an envelope by itself, also a window envelope, and tells you at the top who the post is from and who it is addressed to. All the text of the envelope follows below. Small print, such as on a medicine leaflet or a tea bag tag: Paper Pilot enlarges the picture and increases the contrast when that reads better. If it still does not work well, try Read handwriting (Ctrl+H): the language model often reads small and curved letters better.

Does Paper Pilot say that text recognition is not available? In Windows, go to Settings, Time and language, Language and region. Choose your language, then Language options, and install Optical character recognition (OCR).

For HEIC photos (iPhone) and WebP, Windows needs a free extension from the Microsoft Store: HEIF Image Extensions and WebP Image Extensions.

Scanning: choose Scan (Ctrl+N). Paper Pilot looks for scanners: connected scanners, scanners on the network, and the scanner in your printer. If there are several, you choose one; Paper Pilot remembers your choice. Then you choose what you are scanning: a letter or document, an envelope, or something small with small print (a label, medicine leaflet or tea bag tag). For small print Paper Pilot scans at high resolution. If the scanner has both a glass plate and a document feeder, you choose where the letter is. With the glass you scan page by page: after each page Paper Pilot asks whether there is another page. Paper Pilot then recognises the text, and tells you if a page was crooked or upside down. Save the scan with Ctrl+Shift+S; choose PDF and the text is included invisibly, so the PDF can be searched later.

Paper Pilot does not find a scanner? Check that the scanner or printer is switched on and connected to the computer or on the same network. Add a new scanner in Windows first: Settings, Bluetooth and devices, Printers and scanners.

Printing: choose Print (Ctrl+P). With a scan or photo you first choose: the text, or the scan or photo. Then the normal Windows print window appears, where you choose the printer.

In File Explorer you can also select a PDF, Word document or photo and choose: Open with, Paper Pilot.


4. Check (for people who make documents or websites)

Choose Check (Alt+C) in the main window. Paper Pilot asks what you want to check: a PDF, a Word document (.docx) or a website. For a website, type or paste the address.

You then get a report in the reading window. At the top it says how many problems and points to watch Paper Pilot found, and what is good. Each point says what is wrong, why it is difficult for someone using a screen reader, where it is, and how to fix it. Use Ctrl+K to jump from point to point, Ctrl+S to save the report, Ctrl+P to print it.

What Paper Pilot checks:
- PDF: real text or a scan, strange characters (fonts), structure (tags), title, language, headings, descriptions of pictures, header rows in tables, bookmarks in a long PDF, names of form fields, and security that blocks the screen reader.
- Word: title, language, real headings and text that only looks like a heading, descriptions of pictures, header rows and complicated tables, colour contrast, very small letters, empty lines used for spacing, unclear link texts, lists typed by hand and text boxes. Save an old .doc file as .docx first.
- Website: language, title, alt text of pictures, links and buttons without a name, form fields without a label, headings, a main section (main), zooming, iframes, duplicate ids, tabindex, sound that plays by itself, and colour contrast in the HTML. Paper Pilot looks at the HTML; what appears through JavaScript and colours from CSS are not checked.

Paper Pilot checks a lot, but not everything. Also ask someone who uses a screen reader to try your document or website.


5. The menus

Press Alt to go to the menu bar.

File
- My post: the letters and documents you have read or scanned, newest first, with the date and the sender. Choose one to open it again, also a scan you did not save. You can also remove one, or clear My post completely.
- Reminders: all your reminders. Make a new one, or choose one to remove it.
- Exit (Alt+F4): close Paper Pilot.

Settings
- Language / Taal: Nederlands or English. Paper Pilot switches language right away.
- Appearance: Automatic (like Windows), Light or Dark. When Windows high contrast is on, Paper Pilot always uses the Windows colours.
- My details: your name, address, date of birth, phone number, email address, BSN and IBAN. Paper Pilot fills them in for you in forms. They are stored encrypted on your own computer; only you can read them.
- My signature: sign with a dark pen on white paper and scan it, or choose a photo. Paper Pilot takes out the signature and stores it encrypted. On a form, Paper Pilot puts it at the question for your signature.
- Keep My post: when this is on, Paper Pilot remembers the letters you read (encrypted, on your own computer).
- Language model for handwriting and photos: download the language model, or remove it if it is already there.
- Check for updates at startup: when this is on, Paper Pilot checks once a day for a new version.

Help
- Manual (F1): this manual.
- Guided tour: a short tour of the program.
- Changes: what changed in each version.
- Privacy statement: what Paper Pilot does and does not do with your data.
- Mail the maker: an email to Jaap Wortel in your own mail program, with the version number included.
- More programs by Jaap: other programs by the maker. Choose one to send Jaap an email about it.
- Check for updates: check now whether there is a new version.
- About Paper Pilot: version and maker.


6. Dictation

In every input field of Paper Pilot you can also use a dictation program, such as Speech to Text, Wispr Flow, Dragon NaturallySpeaking or Windows dictation (Windows+H). Paper Pilot never jumps to another field by itself, so your dictation always ends up in the field you are in.

Paper Pilot never uses the Insert key or Caps Lock: those are your screen reader keys. While you hold down one of those keys, Paper Pilot does not react to any key.


7. The first time and after an update

The first time you start Paper Pilot, you see a short guided tour. After an update you see once what is new. You can find both again in the Help menu.


8. Updates

Paper Pilot checks once a day whether there is a new version (you can turn this off in the Settings menu). If there is, Paper Pilot asks whether you want to install it. If you choose Yes, Windows asks for permission. Paper Pilot closes for a moment, the new version is installed and Paper Pilot starts again by itself. The old program files are removed. Your own data and settings are kept.


9. Installing and uninstalling

Paper Pilot is installed in the Program Files folder. Windows asks for permission once.

You can uninstall it in two ways:
- Start, Uninstall Paper Pilot.
- Windows Settings, Apps, Paper Pilot, Uninstall.

When uninstalling, Paper Pilot asks whether your own data and settings should be removed too.


10. Where Paper Pilot stores things

Settings, the log file, My details, My post, your reminders and your signature are in the folder %APPDATA%\Paper Pilot on your own computer. My details, My post, the reminders and the signature are encrypted. The log file helps to find errors. It is never sent to anyone by itself.
